# Rules, with the evidence behind them

Every rule here comes from a real mistake on the source film: a 2.5-minute, 17-chapter vertical explainer built by AI agents. Each entry gives the rule, then the reason, with the numbers measured at the time. Copy this file into the project as `docs/rules.md`. The 20 most important rules are in the project's CLAUDE.md (from the template; SKILL.md shows the same list). A rule that fits only some forms says so ("If the film shows a counter..."); `forms.md` lists which rules depend on the form.

Contents: Timing and sync · Composition and readability · Handoffs and transitions · Captions and safe zones · Lighting and colour · Motion · Facts · Audio · Code · Performance · Orchestration · Output and reuse

## Timing and sync
- **T1. Lock the timing source before building, then build to its measured times.** The timing source is the voice take for a narrated film (the default), the music edit for a music-led film, or the text for a text-only film. Why: chapters built to planned times landed 0.2-0.8 s off on every beat, and re-timing them cost a full extra pass.
- **T2. Use one timing source.** Each chapter has a `beats.ts` of local frames. Every frame elsewhere is `BEATS.x` plus or minus an offset, and each beat is tagged `// sync: "<word>"` or `// free`. Write a deliberate lead or lag as `s(<onset>) + n`, so `scripts/qa/sync_drift.py` can check the onset. Why: hard-coded keys (19.8, 20.4) and a hard-coded `DUR=165` broke when the voice was re-timed. One split window opened before its predecessor, and a clamp wiped a 64-cell stagger.
- **T3. After any re-time, simulate the timeline before rendering.** Assert that windows stay in order, no clamp overrides a designed delay, each hero state holds at least 12 frames, a new action starts only once the previous spring has settled within 5%, and the camera stays until the subject's effect is about 99% done. Why: a close-up shrank to 11 frames, and a split started on a 1.25 spring overshoot.
- **T4. Measure the onsets of important words from the chapter's own clip.** Use the clip offset by its `clipAt`, not a phrase-spread table. Why: word times spread by length inside a phrase were up to 0.3 s late. Measured onsets reached a 22 ms mean error on 73 hand-checked words.
- **T5. If the film shows a counter, change it on the beat that shows the change, and put that beat on the spoken word.** Why: on the source film, day labels ran 1-2 steps ahead of what was on screen, and counter numbers landed before their spoken words in 3 chapters.
- **T6. Pace the script.** Keep the VO at 2.1 words/s or less overall (2.3 per chapter), with one idea per chapter and about 15% of the film without narration. On-screen text with no voice runs at about 3 words a second at most (a starting value, not yet proven on a film). Why: drafts ran at 2.3-2.56 words/s, while the real voice speaks at about 2.15, which put the film over its length cap.
- **T7. Give each beat time.** Each visual beat lasts at least 0.7 s. If the film shows a counter, each SPOKEN value rests at least 1.4 s; unspoken in-between values get about 0.85 s each, or roll through in one quick run, and the counter never flips while a caption still names the old value. Why: beats of about 0.5 s didn't register at feed speed. On the source film, a 1.4 s hold for every value left no room for the unspoken ones (two got about 1.2 s each), and at two joins the counter was about to flip while the previous caption still named the old value.
- **T8. Anchor whatever frame 0 or the END depends on to constants, never to a beat frame.** Layouts and slot assignments, seeds, loop phases (a bob, a pulse, a breath, a blink) and anything sampled "at the pose of beat X" use a fixed frame, `DUR` or a constant, so moving a beat cannot change frame 0 or the END. Why: on the source film, a 64-cell layout took its slots from the pose at a mid-chapter beat, so moving that beat would have re-shuffled 35 of 64 cells in the END, and a bob phase anchored to another beat moved the END by 1.5 px.
- **T9. When the timing source makes a rule impossible, keep the sync and log the exception.** Two synced words (or music beats) can be closer than a rule allows: a 12-frame hold, a 0.7 s beat, a spring's settle time, a counter value's 1.4 s. Keep both beats on their words. Use one move that starts on the first word and completes on the second, or let the second word land on a state already on screen. Tag the beat `// sync: "in" (2.31) [accept: hold 8 f < 12, voice-bound]` and list it under "Accepted exceptions" in RESUME.md. Never move a beat off its word, stretch the chapter or ask for a new take just to meet a rule; a new take is the user's call. When a chain of springs gets less time than one spring needs to settle, start each next move from where the previous one is DRAWN (position = start + the sum of the springs so far), not from its rest target, so nothing snaps. `sync_drift.py` reports `[accept]` beats and never fails on them. Why: on the source film, "gets" and "in" were 7.8 frames apart, and a cascade of splits got 6-7 frames each where its spring needed 11. Reviewers logged the first and chained the springs for the second, and both read cleanly at phone size.

## Composition and readability
- **R1. Pull every beat frame with its short side at 270 px and write "this reads as ___".** That is 270x480 at 9:16, 480x270 at 16:9 and 270x270 at 1:1. Why: this was the #1 repeat mistake. It hit 5 of 5 chapters in a row, even with the rule in CLAUDE.md, so make the sentence a required output of the polish pass.
- **R2. Break the face and animal traps.** Two similar round shapes side by side read as eyes, a dot inside a circle as an eye, and a dark curve under two dots as a mouth. A round head with a dot eye and a coloured bump reads as a chick or duckling, and bulbous tips read as a frog's foot. Slits and capsules can read as body parts. Fix them with diagonal placement, different sizes, asymmetry and no catchlight dots. Why: 3 beads read as a smiley, two clusters as eyes, a head as a duckling, and a smooth capsule finger read as phallic. That last one was the polish pass's biggest fix.
- **R3. The SUBJECT OF THE BEAT takes at least 35% of the frame's short side.** The subject is the thing the beat names (the hand on "fingers", the bar on "doubled"), not the whole character or chart. Plan the camera curve for it, and dim non-focal layers by 40% or more. Why: subjects sat at 16-28% of the width (a 9:16 film), so a hand was 42 px wide on a phone.
- **R4. Minimum readable size.** Anything that carries a beat needs a core of at least 8 px per 1080 px of short side, plus a halo of 3x or more. Lines and windows need at least 40 px. Why: 1-2 px particles disappeared on a 270 px-wide phone screen.
- **R5. Every narrated (or written) noun or verb is visibly on screen on its word.** Make the checklist from the narration and pull the frame at each word. Why: one narrated layer never lit up until polish caught it.
- **R6. Draw the real thing.** Write the 2-3 features that identify an object before you draw it. Avoid uniform circles, evenly spaced rows, stroke-only clip-art and lens-flare stand-ins. Why: a "glass box" read as ice, a two-line cord read as a ladder, pulse rings stacked into a target, and survivors lined up like comb teeth.
- **R7. Show at most 3 shockwave or pulse rings at once,** each a thin crest (7-12 px) plus a soft trail. Concentric backdrop rings read as a target.
- **R8. One focal point per beat.** Story logic must read numerically: whoever "wins" is visibly in the lead at the beat frame. Why: the "only one wins" runner arrived LAST.
- **R9. Objects on the same depth plane share one blur,** and draw order follows the real spatial story. Why: a sharp object sat next to a 30 px-blurred one, and a wrong draw order made an object seem to travel backwards.
- **R10. The hook.** Frame 0 is a clean thumbnail. Motion starts outside the platform UI zones by frame 5 (the upper 75% at 9:16; above the caption band at 16:9 and 1:1), sound starts by 0.1 s, and the first camera key sits at frame -30. Frames 1-15 should have a mean frame diff at or above the film median. Why: the first 20 frames were almost still (diff 0.2-0.3 against a median of 1.05), and the first motion sat under the platform UI. The fix lifted upper-frame motion from 0.29 to 0.77.

## Handoffs and transitions
- **H1. When the subject continues across a join (fade, focus, or a match cut), the upstream owns the END contract, written as numbers.** It covers centre, radius, zoom, light stops, seeds, clock offset, breathing phase, facing and sides. Write it in `src/film/handoffs.ts` (numbers the code imports) and `briefs/ends/Cxx.md` (prose for agents); the downstream chapter starts from it. A hard cut to a new scene has no contract: check only that no layer is mid-fade on either side of it. Why: across 10 handoffs the lighting, size, zoom, facing and seeds differed, rims doubled, sizes jumped and colours popped.
- **H2. Diff against the REAL upstream frame, never a placeholder.** Target a mean of 2/255 or less and a max of 20/255 or less, only in blurred areas. Why: two agents chased each other's stale renders.
- **H3. Neighbours change while you work.** The downstream builder matches the upstream's CURRENT END, and its polish re-checks the NUMBERS against the upstream's FINAL END (re-copy any shared clock or lineage file); the pixel diff runs once, in the joins pass. Why: an upstream changed its tilt while the downstream was copying it.
- **H4. Remotion's `fade()` keeps the outgoing scene fully opaque** and lays the new one on top. Fade outgoing translucent extras (glows, halos, rings, shells) linearly from 1 to 0 over the last 15 frames, and let the downstream redraw them. Why: halos doubled during the overlap and vanished at the cut.
- **H5. Use `focusPull`** (old shot defocuses, new pulls into focus) whenever two similar silhouettes would crossfade. A plain crossfade between them ghosts.
- **H6. Keep the subject still through the 15-frame overlap.** Verify on the composite from END-20 to END+5, because an isolated preview looks wrong there by design.
- **H7. Keep the total light area continuous** when shapes split, merge or swap (one blob into two, one icon morphing into another). Why: a light sized from the sum of two lobes popped, and a double heart showed during a swap.

## Captions and safe zones
(S1 and S5-S7 apply with captions on, `FILM.captions` in `src/film/config.ts`. Bands are `CAPTION_BAND` and `TRACKER_BAND` of the chosen `ASPECT` in `src/theme/layout.ts`; the numbers below are the 9:16 ones.)
- **S1. The caption band (y 1300-1560, x 150-930) stays calm.** Render without captions and measure: median contrast against the caption colour (`C.text`) of at least 4.5:1 on every frame (aim for 6:1), and mean luma about 65/255 or lower under light text (about 190 or higher under dark text). Why: on the key line, contrast was 2.1-3.6:1. The fixes raised the worst case from 2.55:1 to 6.44:1.
- **S2. Bright events get a multiply scrim that reaches full strength at least 4 frames BEFORE the light ramps.** Why: a scrim that eased in with the bloom arrived about 0.7 s late. Multiply never shows as a dark bar.
- **S3. Hot light cores stay well clear of the caption band:** below it (y 1900 or more) or 100 px above it (y 1200 or less; screen y grows downward). Effects fade to 0 by y 1290 in screen space.
- **S4. If the film shows a counter, the top band (y 100-290) stays clear under it.** Compute the hero's bounds at the camera's MAX zoom (`worldToScreen` in `src/lib/camera.tsx`), and if bright detail crosses the band, add a top shade driven by the same camera curve. Give screen-space shades an 8-stop eased falloff, because a 2-stop shade showed a hard line.
- **S5. Captions never stack.** `fadeEnd = min(end+16, nextFirstWord-3)`, `fadeStart = min(end+6, fadeEnd-6)`: a word starts fading in 2 frames before its onset, so this leaves 2 empty frames. Why: lines 0.33 s apart overlapped, and a later pass on the source film still found 1-frame gaps between lines in one chapter.
- **S6. Use 2 rows at most at the caption size (76 px at 9:16),** and page longer sentences at phrase breaks, preferring punctuation. Why: 9 sentences wrapped to 3-6 rows.
- **S7. The last line is an end title:** bigger (about 112 px at 9:16), one line, the accent colour on the key word, held to the final frame with a slow 4% scale-up. Why: a normal subtitle that faded out before the end made a weak ending.
- **S8. Every word on screen earns its place, and text in the art follows the caption rules.** In a narrated, picture-led film, keep text out of the art (captions, plus the counter if there is one), because every label competes with the captions. Where labels ARE the picture (a chart's axes and values, a map's place names, a diagram's parts, a product's UI, kinetic type), treat each label like a caption. It appears on its word or beat. Its font size is at least 40 px per 1080 px of frame width, and it passes the 270 px read test. It has 4.5:1 contrast or better against what is behind it. At most one new block of text (up to 2 labels) appears at a time. It stays out of the caption band and the platform UI zones. It holds for its reading time: about 3 words a second at most, and never under 0.7 s. Why: on the source film, chip texts that changed faster than anyone could read them had to be skipped by a hold rule, and caption lines 0.33 s apart overlapped.

## Lighting and colour
These rules assume screen-blend glows on a dark backdrop, the template's default style. Why: the source film used glowing flat 2D. Isometric, paper and line-art styles shade with flat tones instead; L1 and L3 still apply (`forms.md`, "Style families").
- **L1. Shade with the same hue.** Shadows on warm forms use a darker tone of the same family, never the cool background colour. Why: gold cells turned olive, gold over plum turned khaki, and a dark scrim over yellow made a brown ellipse.
- **L2. Screen or add to brighten; multiply to darken.** Never screen a colour onto itself (red on red turns pale pink). When dimming, use a darker tone of the SAME hue or a near-neutral. A blue multiply over cyan turns royal blue, and a dark normal-blend overlay turns gold khaki.
- **L3. After every lighting change, sample the hero's pixels.** Olive, khaki, brown or grey means the blend is wrong.
- **L4. Focal colours stay saturated at their peak.** Draw the focal shape after its glow, with a smaller flare.

## Motion
- **M1. Never `return null` on a visible layer;** fade it out over 6-10 frames. Why: a full-frame halo removal caused a 79/255 step, which the fix brought down to 21.
- **M2. Scan for luma steps.** Any frame-to-frame step above 20/255 in the art region, outside an intended flash or a designed cut, needs a reason (`luma_steps.py --film` lists the designed cuts as CUT, not POP).
- **M3. Never toggle a filter on a group whose children use blend modes.** It changes blend isolation and jumps for one frame. Move blended layers outside filtered groups.
- **M4. Nothing moves linearly.** Use the house easings and springs, with anticipation, overshoot and follow-through. Peaks get S-curves with a held beat, never ease-in into a peak and expo out of it.
- **M5. Alive, but still while something is read.** In picture-led styles, use a slow camera drift or push (scale about 1.00 to 1.06 per chapter), noise-driven wobble, and parallax layers where far means smaller, blurrier and slower (`src/lib/camera.tsx`). While a chart, a map, a label or a line of type is being read, hold the camera still (`drift={0}`): the life comes from the build. Why: on the source film (picture-led), near-still opening frames read as frozen (R10). The hold-still half is a starting rule for label-led forms, not yet proven on a film.
- **M6. Check rigs at their extreme poses.** Why: a far leg crept out during a wind-up and showed a 7-toe foot.

## Facts (details in accuracy.md)
- **F1.** Store every fact as {claim, convention, primary source}. Run a separate adversarial fact-check after research. Why: 29 claims were rejected and 6 data-table fixes made.
- **F2.** Use one convention, stated once on screen or in the VO, and convert every source to it.
- **F3.** Put no number or percentage on screen without a primary source. Hedge it ("about 8 in 10"); no overprecise numbers.
- **F4.** Comparisons ("the size of a lime", "as tall as a bus") must match the numbers and only ever grow when the quantity grows. Check them with a script.
- **F5.** Every polish pass lists each on-screen claim and visual proportion against the facts and proportion tables.

## Audio (details in audio.md)
- **A1.** Every SFX file starts at sample 0.000 with a single onset. Apply a 3-10 ms fade-in AFTER filters, put `asetpts=PTS-STARTPTS` right after `atrim`, and work in 32-bit float. Why: a take started at -0.512 (a -10.5 dBFS jump), and `afade` silently did nothing without `asetpts`.
- **A2.** Tails decay below -60 dBFS or end with a 30 ms fade.
- **A3.** On every word, the voice beats all SFX by at least 6 dB in 1-4 kHz and by at least 2 LU of momentary loudness. Why: a riser at 0.6 sat 7-12 dB OVER the key line's consonants.
- **A4.** Every SFX sits within about 10 dB of the voice through a 200 Hz high-pass (a phone model), so it needs content at 120-600 Hz. Why: a kick and an impact had 51-66% of their energy below 80 Hz and vanished on phones.
- **A5.** `volume={(f) => ...}` counts clip-local frames, and ducks land about 2 frames late, so start them about 0.07 s early.
- **A6.** Normalise the VO to -16 LUFS (linear two-pass), and master the film to -14 LUFS / -1.5 dBTP. `master.sh` limits peaks over the linear budget first, then refuses a missed target (it writes `*-MISSED.mp4` and exits 1).
- **A7.** Cut voice clips at the lowest-energy point of a real pause, then transcribe every clip to verify it. Why: 4 of 16 clips held a neighbour's words.

## Code
- **C1.** Name ids `c05-<component>-<name>` (the lower-case chapter id first), add `useId()` inside kits, and define each filter or gradient once per page. Why: one id used for both a gradient and a filter, and a filter defined in two files, meant effects never rendered.
- **C2.** Append alpha to 6-digit hex only. Why: an `rgba(...)` string with a hex alpha appended was invalid, so a key light never drew, and the next scene then matched the broken look.
- **C3.** When an effect looks weak, first confirm that it renders at all.
- **C4.** Use deterministic randomness only: `random(seed)` and `@remotion/noise`, never `Math.random()` or `Date`. Use colours from palette `C` only.
- **C5.** Keep files at 300 lines or fewer, split by job (`Camera.tsx`, `beats.ts`, `Subject.tsx`, `index.tsx`).
- **C6.** Copy finished pilot code into a chapter; never import a finished scene. Better: build pilot scenes as components that take a `beats` prop and size, so a longer cut mounts them directly. Why: a 30 s pilot hard-wired to global timing had to be copied and re-adapted for 7 chapters.
- **C7.** When a kit lacks a feature, add it to the kit's API; never copy the kit's internals.

## Performance
- **P1.** Keep to the agent budget (2 agents at a time by default, in two lanes: `orchestration.md`), one render each, under `nice -n 15`. Why: on the source film's 10-core Mac, 5-6 agents rendering pushed the load to 48-70, and 0.5-scale previews went from 11-20 s to 110-330 s.
- **P2.** Preview at scale 0.4 and iterate on range renders (`FRAMES=a-b`). Render full resolution only for stills and the final render.
- **P3.** Give `overflow: hidden` to any masked or blurred layer that scales far past the frame (WIDTH x HEIGHT). Why: Chrome "Page crashed!" 3 times out of 3 on a layer scaled about 10x past the frame.
- **P4.** Use 5 or fewer full-frame blur layers per chapter. Prefer one filter per group, CSS blur on a group, or radial gradients for glow, over hundreds of SVG filter instances.
- **P5.** Kill orphaned `chrome-headless-shell` processes with `scripts/kill-chrome.sh`, which refuses while a CLI render is running (finish or cancel any render started from Remotion Studio first).

## Orchestration (details in orchestration.md)
- **O1.** Builder, then independent polish. Never trust the builder's report.
- **O2.** QA lens, then a skeptic verify, then a fix with the `betterFix`. Why: 13 of 15 confirmed findings needed a corrected fix, 3 of 19 were refuted outright, and metric scales differed about 3.5x between agents. Use the fixed probes in `scripts/qa/`.
- **O3.** Only the orchestrator, or the handoff agent acting for it between chapters, edits shared files. Each agent has its own scratch folder. Why: agents overwrote each other's helper scripts, and every chapter edited the registry.
- **O4.** Workflows are resumable. An agent writes a progress note after each render cycle, and a retry prompt says "continue from the partial work". Why: 7 builders died on connection errors, and 4 polish passes ended with no result.
- **O5.** Agents read a one-page chapter pack, not the full briefs. Run the pixel-level join pass once at the end. Why: the same quality bar was reached with less waiting.
- **O6.** Keep QA prompts read-only and scoped. Agents ignore relayed requests that have nothing to do with their task.
- **O7. Agents never rewrite git state in a shared tree.** No `git stash`, `git checkout -- <path>`, `git restore`, `git reset` or `git clean`: other agents have uncommitted work in it. The orchestrator commits before launching agents, so `git diff -- src/film/chapters/<id>` shows exactly what each agent changed. Why: on the source film a re-timer stashed its chapter to render "before" stills while another agent's uncommitted chapter sat in the same tree; a wider stash would have taken that work.

## Output and reuse
- **U1.** Decide the `out/` layout before the first render and bake it into the scripts. Every prompt says where scratch goes and asks the agent to delete it when done. Why: `out/` grew to 1,383 flat files (1 GB).
- **U2.** Before reporting done, grep the chapter for imports of finished scenes and for leftover `_qa` preview entries.
- **U3.** Use git from day one, and commit after each finished chapter, so regressions can be diffed.
